# Live sweep · lists, reports and import (LED-125) — retro (2026-09-26)

Tree tested: branch `epics-8-13-phase-12` at `a2dde39` (`4ab1888` plus the LED-124 retro; no source changed). Lint, build and all 666 tests were green on `4ab1888` before the phase.

## Method
Same as LED-124 (`knowledge/patterns/browser-check-with-local-user.md`): local Supabase only, headless Chrome over CDP, throwaway users. This sweep used `sweep-b@example.test`, seeded over `psql` with **2,000 transactions on one account** ("Big Checking": 1,500 expenses in 2026 inside a yearly Groceries budget, 200 incomes, 300 older expenses; the oldest is 2025-06-01), a monthly Dining budget, and later 22 extra categories (33 with activity in September) for the breakdown. `sweep-empty@example.test` covered the no-records states. A fresh **160-row test CSV** (BPI-style `Date,Description,Amount`, D/M/Y, 3 planted duplicates, 3 unparseable dates including a footer "Total", 1 bad amount, 1 empty description, 15 unknown payees, 3 transfer-wording rows) and a 3,000-row CSV were generated in the scratchpad. Viewports 1920x1080, 1280x900, 1024x768, 768x1024, 390x844 and 375x812; light and dark. Performance was measured on the Vite dev server (React dev build) and repeated on `vite preview` of a production build, with a `PerformanceObserver` for long tasks. Screenshots and scripts stay in the session scratchpad.

**LED-149 item 9 (virtualisation) — decision.** Needed, at least as memoisation. Every load step re-renders every mounted row, so its cost grows with the list: on a production build the longest task per step passes 50 ms at about 420 rows on desktop (480 at 390), 100 ms at about 1,100 rows (780 at 390) and reaches 190 ms (470 ms at 390) at 2,000 rows, with 58,700 DOM nodes and 300 to 450 MB of heap. Steady scrolling of a fully mounted list is smooth. On a phone CPU the late steps would freeze for one to three seconds. Suggested scope: memoise `TransactionRow` so a step renders only the new rows; virtualise only if that is not enough.

Side effect of the sweep: while cleaning up after the import test a `like '% purchase #9%'` delete also removed about 120 seeded rows (the account went from 2,000 to 1,878 rows before the import). Numbers measured before that point were taken on the full 2,000.
