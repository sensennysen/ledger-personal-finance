# A card payment is saved as a transfer into the card

The database rejects an expense whose destination is not a loan (`enforce_loan_repayment_destination`), and `update_account_balance` credits an expense's `to_account_id` only when it is a loan. A payment to a credit card is therefore a `transfer` from the paying account to the card: no category, rate 1, same currency (`cardPaymentTransfer` in `src/lib/cardPayment.ts`).

**Why:** LED-24 built the card form as "expense with a destination" and never saved one in a browser; the LED-113 retro found it could not be saved at all, and LED-146 moved it onto the working path.

**How to apply:**
1. Every save of a card payment goes through `useCardPayment().createWithStatement`, so the payment record and the statement's paid amount follow the transfer. That covers any transfer into a credit card, whichever form made it.
2. `transferCard(values, accounts)` says whether a save is one. Do not test `type === 'expense' && to_account_id` for a card.
3. LED-114 (OD-2 b) kept this model: no Card payments category and no migration. Because a card payment is a transfer, it stays out of every spending total, which all count `type === 'expense'` (`tests/cardPaymentSpending.test.mjs` pins it). Nothing may write an expense to a card, and a new spending calculation must filter on `type === 'expense'` too.
4. Recurring transfers to a card are generated by `generateDueRecurring`, which does not run the statement steps (Backlog in the LED-146 retro).
